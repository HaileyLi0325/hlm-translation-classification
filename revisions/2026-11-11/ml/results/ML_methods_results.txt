MACHINE LEARNING REVISION METHODS AND RESULTS

Final frozen source corpus: 240 chapters and 722 chapter-matched 1,500-regex-word segments (361 per edition); all 120 source chapters represented.



R4.2 and R5.7 Sampling sensitivity

For each chapter, k was the smaller of the two editions' complete 1,500-word window counts. Every scheme retained k disjoint windows in each edition.

The prefix scheme reproduces the canonical fixed-length feature matrix. Position-stratified offsets span zero to the unused word count evenly; a single retained window is centered.

Three random-gap schemes used seeds 101, 202 and 303. A chapter/edition-specific SeedSequence generated k discrete uniform offsets between zero and the unused word count. Sorted offsets plus j*1500 yielded nonoverlapping windows.

Five source-chapter folds (KFold, shuffle=True, random_state=42) were identical across all schemes and algorithms. Both editions and every window from a chapter remained together.

All 18 source-defined entropy features and all eight original classifiers were reused at their original defaults. Hard voting combined their labels; ties selected Hawkes-Minford.

Values below are means and sample SDs of the five fold accuracies, not confidence intervals or independent significance tests.

position_stratified: hard-voting accuracy 94.7498% (fold SD 1.4331 percentage points).

prefix: hard-voting accuracy 94.0330% (fold SD 1.9704 percentage points).

random_101: hard-voting accuracy 92.9586% (fold SD 2.2506 percentage points).

random_202: hard-voting accuracy 93.3602% (fold SD 1.1717 percentage points).

random_303: hard-voting accuracy 94.7595% (fold SD 1.9989 percentage points).

Alternative-minus-prefix accuracy range: -1.0745 to 0.7265 percentage points.

hawkes: mean fraction of regex words retained per chapter 64.7239%, range 46.3106% to 90.8981%.

yang: mean fraction of regex words retained per chapter 85.8216%, range 67.2495% to 99.6899%.

Unequal coverage is retained and explicitly acknowledged. These experiments examine segment position sensitivity, not equivalence of content spans or coverage proportions.



R5.8 Held-out performance

A seed-42 NumPy generator selected 24 of 120 source chapters without replacement; the remaining 96 chapters were used for training. Every corresponding edition observation remained in the same partition.

Macro precision, macro recall, macro F1, and specificity were calculated from test predictions. Specificity denotes recall for Hawkes-Minford with Yang-Yang defined as positive.

ROC-AUC used class probability or decision_function scores where provided. Hard voting has no probability/decision score, so its AUC is not reported.

All metric confidence intervals are percentile intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of complete held-out source-chapter groups (seed 20261111). Both editions and all nested segments travel together.

These intervals describe empirical test-group variability conditional on the fitted models. They exclude corpus selection and model-training uncertainty; boundary estimates can have degenerate intervals.

window_1500: train n=580, test n=142; hard-voting accuracy 92.9577% (95% CI 88.2801-97.1831%), macro precision 93.2667%, macro recall 92.9577%, macro F1 92.9452%, specificity 97.1831%.

chapter_level: train n=192, test n=48; hard-voting accuracy 97.9167% (95% CI 93.7500-100.0000%), macro precision 98.0000%, macro recall 97.9167%, macro F1 97.9158%, specificity 95.8333%.



R5.9 Stability

Stability is operationalized as the sample SD of grouped-fold test accuracy and the mean train-test accuracy gap. Smaller values indicate less fold variability or less apparent overfitting, respectively.

On prefix sampling, voting has lower fold SD than 6 of 8 constituent models and a smaller train-test gap than 4 of 8.

Across five sampling schemes, voting has lower SD in 24/40 comparisons and a lower train-test gap in 21/40. It does not universally improve stability.



R5.10 SHAP and predictor correlations

New SHAP analyses replace the unverifiable historical SHAP outputs. Each 500-tree Random Forest was trained on the 96 training chapters and explained only held-out observations (48 chapters or 142 segments).

TreeExplainer used tree_path_dependent perturbation and the training path counts stored in the fitted trees. The explained class was Yang-Yang; positive SHAP values increase its fitted probability.

The expected value plus the SHAP sum was checked against Random Forest probability for every explained observation. Maximum absolute error was 3.11e-15.

chapter_level mean absolute SHAP top features: word_trigram_entropy=0.112884, pos_unigram_entropy=0.097791, punctuation_entropy=0.086816, clause_marker_entropy=0.042852.

window_1500 mean absolute SHAP top features: word_bigram_entropy=0.118242, word_trigram_entropy=0.109652, punctuation_entropy=0.062888, pos_unigram_entropy=0.053520.

Predictors are strongly correlated even within editions. Chapter-level word-bigram/word-trigram Spearman rho is approximately 0.978 in Hawkes-Minford and 0.983 in Yang-Yang.

Segment wordform/lemma entropy correlations are approximately 0.964 and 0.958, respectively. Contributions may therefore be shared or redistributed among correlated predictors.

SHAP describes this fitted model; it does not establish a unique linguistic mechanism or causal determinants of edition differences.
